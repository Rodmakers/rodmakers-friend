Rodmaker's Friend

Bamboo fly rod plan, build & track — one file, in your browser. No install, no account, no internet needed once you have it.

What it isn't

It isn't a taper design program and doesn't try to be. It won't draw a stress curve or tell you what line a taper wants. Design or check your taper in whatever you already use — Hexrod, RodDNA, CaneCalc, DynaRod — then paste the station dimensions in here and this takes over from there.

What it does

Paste in a taper and it works out what you need at the bench:

Planing form depths and Morgan Hand Mill settings at every station, in your units, with your own oversize.
Rough and final targets worked out from how you get there — whether you cut a rough taper or bevel to a straight triangle, whether you dry bind and check for gaps before final, whether the enamel is still on when you plane.
Strip length, from your rod length and pieces and how much extra you like at each end, checked against how far your equipment actually reaches.
A strip planner by section, because a culm doesn't give up as many butt strips as tip strips.
A section cut list that accounts for the female ferrule standing proud of the cane and the male slide disappearing inside it — bare cane or finished section, depending on when you cut.
Ferrule sizing to the next 64th, at the station where you actually put the joint.
Hollow-building — add a third column to your taper and it graphs the hollow and tells you where it runs out.
A build checklist that assembles itself from your choices and prints with the rod's name on it.
A rod database — every rod you've planned or built, with how far through each one you are.
Nothing is pre-chosen

There isn't a single default in it. Every pull-down starts blank. It doesn't assume you flame or don't, that you stagger nodes one way or another or work nodeless, that you heat treat in an oven or over a flame, that you use a mill or forms or both, that you soak or you don't. It asks, and then follows your answer through everything downstream.

Same with your shop. You start by checking the equipment you actually own, and from then on it only offers paths you can build.

Getting it running

Mac or PC — open the link above, or save the file and double-click it.

Android — open the link, or save the file and open it with Chrome.

iPhone or iPad — use the link. If you'd rather have the file, tapping it in Mail shows a dead page because Apple's quick preview won't run it; save it to Files or iCloud Drive and open it with any free HTML viewer from the App Store.

Full instructions are built into the page itself — see How to use Rodmaker's Friend at the top.

Your rods

Rods are stored by your browser, on your device. Nothing is uploaded anywhere and nothing is shared.

That is not a backup. Clearing browsing data will erase it. Use 💾 Save all rods in My Rods to write everything out to a file you keep somewhere backed up, and 📂 Restore rods to bring it back. Restoring merges rather than overwrites, so it's safe to do whenever you're unsure.

Each device keeps its own copy — there's no sync. Move rods between machines with a saved file.

This is a beta

I'd like makers to actually build a rod with it, or plan one, and tell me where it's wrong. Particularly:

Anywhere it assumes something that isn't universal. That's the whole point of the thing and it's where I most expect to have failed.
Anywhere the numbers don't match what you'd work out yourself — tell me the taper, the station, and what you got.
Anywhere it offers something your equipment can't do, or hides something it can.
Vocabulary. If I've called something by a name you don't use, or attached a maker's name to a method that isn't his, I want to know.
